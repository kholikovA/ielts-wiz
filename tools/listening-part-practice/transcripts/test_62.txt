[147.5] Having said all that, what I'm about to tell you will apply equally to any audience.
[154.9] So, how do you structure your presentation?
[158.9] Right at the beginning, you should tell them something that forces them to [[32|pay attention]].
[164.2] This could be something surprising or even shocking, but it needs to be relevant.
[171.9] After that, you need a list of items or topics showing them what you'll be covering, rather like an agenda.
[180.6] And then the main part of the presentation will follow.
[184.7] This main part will be the detailed information you'll be presenting and could include facts, statistics, personal experiences, etc.
[195.9] After this, you should summarise what you've presented and close with what I call [[33|next steps]].
[204.1] For this assignment, you could simply point the group to other sociology reference material.

[210.6] In other cases, you may want to suggest some actions that people can take.
[216.4] Now, what about the design of the slides for your laptop?
[221.7] Well, the important thing here is to be [[34|consistent]].
[226.3] You need to have the same type of font and use the same colour and size for the same elements.
[233.7] For example, all headers need to look the same.
[237.9] All bullet points need to be presented in the same way.
[241.8] And don't just stick to words.
[244.4] Bring the presentation to life by adding [[35|graphics]].
[248.4] These could be in several forms, such as pictures, flowcharts, diagrams, histograms, and so on.

[258.5] And so, let's move on now to presenting.
[261.9] You have your presentation prepared, and you're ready to start.
[266.5] Well, it's important to give a good impression from the start.
[271.4] So take three deep breaths, look at the audience, no matter how frightening they may be, and be enthusiastic and energetic.
[281.9] As you go through the presentation, remember to provide some variety in the way you speak.
[288.2] So, for example, you can talk fairly rapidly for information that may be familiar, but then slow down for more unfamiliar sections.
[298.8] And change your [[36|tone]] as you speak.
[301.4] Don't keep it at the same level all the way through.
[307.0] As I mentioned, look at your audience.

[311.4] A good tip is to pick people out and look at them for around five seconds.
[317.7] Not looking at the audience gives the impression that you're either not interested in them or terrified of them.
[324.8] Looking too long at one particular person may make them feel rather uncomfortable.
[331.2] There may be points in your presentation that you want your audience to really absorb, and in order to make important points stand out, you may consider adding [[37|silence]] right after these.
[345.5] It will give people time to reflect on what you've just said.
[350.8] Also, you may be presenting complicated ideas or technical details, but try to keep everything as simple as possible.
[360.9] Use simple words, and as few as possible.
[365.5] And be clear.
[367.4] If you say something like, this appears to be, it implies uncertainty.

[372.7] So using [[38|weak verbs]] such as appears, seems, could be, etc. needs to be avoided.
[382.6] I'll just finish off with a few thoughts on questions and interruptions from the audience.
[388.6] You may choose to invite questions from the audience as you go, or ask them to wait until the end.
[396.1] Either way, questions should be encouraged, as it provides you with some feedback on how interested the audience is and how well they're understanding you.
[406.5] When a question is asked, you need to provide an answer that is as accurate as possible.
[412.9] So, initially, my tip is to [[39|repeat it]].
[417.0] This will ensure you have heard it correctly and will give you a few seconds to gather your thoughts.

[424.6] Interruptions, on the other hand, can be unwelcome, and you may get them for a variety of reasons.
[431.6] It's likely, however, that there's something in your presentation that's unclear or confusing.
[437.6] So, my advice is to reduce problems by reading through your presentation beforehand and [[40|predicting]] potential points which could cause interruptions.
[447.4] You may then want to change that part of your presentation, or at least you'll be prepared if someone does interrupt you.

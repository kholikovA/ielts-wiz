[83.2] My topic is handedness: whether in different sports it is better to be left or right-sided or whether a more balanced approach is more successful.
[95.2] I'm left-handed myself, and I actually didn't see any relevance to my own life when I happened to start reading an article by a sports psychologist called Peter Matthews.
[107.1] He spent the first part of the article talking about handedness in music instead of sport, which I have to say almost put me off from reading further.
[117.3] But what I soon became struck by was the [[31|sheer volume of both observation and investigation he had done]] in many different sports, and I felt persuaded that what he had to say would be of real interest.
[132.7] I think Matthews' findings will be beneficial, not so much in helping sportspeople to work on their weaker side, but more that they can help them [[32|identify the most suitable strategies to use in a given game]].
[148.2] Although most trainers know how important handedness is, at present they are [[33|rather reluctant to make use of the insights scientists like Matthews can give]], which I think is rather short-sighted, because focusing on individual flexibility is only part of the story.
[167.8] Anyway, back to the article.
[170.9] Matthews found a German study which looked at what he called mixed-handedness, that is, the capacity to use both left and right hands equally.
[181.9] It looked at mixed-handedness in 40 musicians on a variety of instruments.
[188.2] Researchers examined a number of variables, for example, type of instrument played, regularity of practice undertaken, and length of time playing instrument, and found the following.

[202.1] Keyboard players had high levels of mixed-handedness, whereas string players like cellists and violinists strongly favoured one hand.
[212.3] Also, [[34|those who started younger were more mixed-handed]].

[218.6] Matthews also reports studies of handedness in apes.
[223.0] Apes get a large proportion of their food by fishing ants from anthills.
[228.6] The studies show that apes, like humans, show handedness,
[234.0] though for them right- and left-handedness is about equal,
[238.3] whereas about 85% of humans are right-handed.
[244.0] Studies showed that [[35|apes consistently using the same hand]]
[248.4] fished out 30% more ants than those varying between the two.

[258.6] Matthews started researching several different sports and found different types of handedness in each.
[265.5] By the way, he uses handedness to refer to the dominant side for feet and eyes as well as hands.
[273.4] Anyway, his team measured the hand, feet, and eyes of 2,611 players and found that there were really three main types of laterality.
[287.4] Mixed, you work equally well on both sides, both hand and eye.
[292.6] Single, you tend to favour one side, but both hand and eye favour the same side.
[300.0] And cross-laterality, a player's hands and eyes favour only one side, but they are opposite sides.
[309.6] Let's start with hockey.
[311.9] Matthews found that it was best to be mixed-handed.
[315.2] This is because a hockey stick must be deployed in [[36|two directions]].

[321.2] It would be a drawback to have hand or eye favouring one side.
[327.1] An interesting finding is that mixed-handed hockey players were significantly more [[37|confident]] than their single-handed counterparts.
[336.8] Things are slightly different in racket sports like tennis.
[341.5] Here, the important thing is to have the dominant hand and eye on the one side.
[348.7] This means that there is a bigger area of [[38|vision]] on the side where most of the action occurs.
[355.2] If a player is cross-lateral, the racket is invisible from the dominant eye for much of the swing.
[362.9] It means that they can only make [[39|corrections]] much later, and often the damage has been done by then.
[369.4] And moving to a rather different type of sport, which involves large but precise movements, gymnastics.
[377.4] It's been found that cross hand-eye favouring is best.

[382.3] The predominant reason for this is because it aids [[40|balance]],
[386.7] which is, of course, absolutely central to performance in this sport.
